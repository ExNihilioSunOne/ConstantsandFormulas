## 2. The Archival Development Method
The archive was not written linearly from the beginning. It was recovered in stages.

### 2.1 Stage One: Raw Intake Preservation
New material first entered as raw data, exploratory blocks, symbolic ideas, decimal observations, approximation attempts, and partially formed structures.

Principle:
Preserve first. Interpret later.

Main purpose:
To prevent loss of seeds, side branches, and promising structures before proper classification.

### 2.2 Stage Two: Recovery and Extraction
Each raw block was examined for its strongest recoverable mathematical content.

Main tasks:
- identify exact symbolic cores,
- separate valid structure from speculative framing,
- reduce overloaded expressions to cleaner seeds or laws,
- preserve strong branches while isolating weaker branches.

### 2.3 Stage Three: Verification Discipline
Recovered material was entered into a truth-status system.

Main verification classes:
- exact identity,
- exact definition,
- approximate numerical relation,
- structurally valid framework,
- exploratory candidate,
- unsupported overclaim.

### 2.4 Stage Four: Theory Formation
When multiple recovered notes displayed the same structural pattern, those notes were unified under a general theory.

### 2.5 Stage Five: Atlas Construction
Once a theory became stable enough, a corresponding atlas was built to catalog concrete entries.

### 2.6 Stage Six: Interconnection and Checksum Synthesis
When repeated values, anchors, asymmetry controls, residues, and invariants began to connect multiple frameworks, an interconnection layer was created.

### 2.7 Stage Seven: Publication Architecture
Once the archive became structurally mature, release-facing documents were produced to support formal recordkeeping and future public dissemination.

## 3. Main Framework Classes
The archive now contains several major framework classes.

### 3.1 Linear Remainder Frameworks
Core form:
R(a,b,c;s) = as + b - cPi

Function:
To compare seed-generated expressions against Pi-layers and track transport, symmetry, and closure behavior.

Main features:
- Pi-level remainder analysis,
- 2Pi and 3Pi transport,
- sign reversal,
- seed comparison,
- closure shifts.

### 3.2 Lower–Upper Anchored Pair Frameworks
Core form:
L + U = A

Function:
To generate paired approximants around Pi under the control of an anchor near 2Pi.

Main features:
- midpoint law,
- asymmetry law,
- forced upper companion,
- anchor-controlled balance.

### 3.3 Cancellation-Pair Frameworks
Core idea:
Two expressions are designed so one term family cancels and a cleaner residue remains.

Main features:
- sum cancellation,
- difference cancellation,
- sign-opposite closure,
- scaffold-versus-residue logic,
- closure to zero, Pi-shift, or integer target.

### 3.4 Transform-Invariant Frameworks
Core idea:
A transform family generated from a seed exhibits exact product or ratio laws.

Main features:
- product invariants,
- ratio invariants,
- internal coherence certification,
- transform checksums.

### 3.5 Reciprocal Cascade Frameworks
Core idea:
Near-unity structures can be modeled through complement errors, halving relations, and reciprocal normalization.

Main features:
- complement descent,
- reciprocal duality,
- normalized coherence,
- one-parameter error models.

### 3.6 Bridge-Value and Checksum Frameworks
Core idea:
Some values recur across frameworks or control the behavior of multiple structures.

Main features:
- bridge remainders,
- midpoint controls,
- anchor errors,
- closure residues,
- exact invariant checksums.

## 4. Core Systematics of the Archive
The archive is systematic because it follows a repeatable structural grammar.

### 4.1 Seed -> Structure -> Relation
A seed is chosen or recovered.
That seed generates an expression or family.
That family is studied through relation to Pi, an anchor, a scaffold companion, or a transform law.

### 4.2 Relation -> Classification -> Reuse
A relation is classified as remainder, anchored pair, cancellation pair, reciprocal pair, invariant, or bridge value.
Once classified, it becomes reusable under theory and atlas layers.

### 4.3 Reuse -> Interconnection -> Synthesis
When a value or law appears in multiple places, it becomes part of the interconnection layer.
This creates the synthesis network.

### 5.1 Seeds

Values or laws certifying coherence, acting as bridges, or controlling framework behavior.
